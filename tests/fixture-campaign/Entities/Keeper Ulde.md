# Keeper Ulde

Toll-keeper of the weir-gate, the only dry crossing into the lower fen. Pleasant the way a
ledger is pleasant; turns a coin he never looks at. Stands at the weir-gate, answers to the
Weir-Keepers' Charter.

## Why it is here
The Charter posts a keeper wherever the fen takes more than it gives; Ulde asked for this gate
the year his brother stopped answering letters.

## What it represents
Complicity priced as duty — an institution letting a man guard his own family's crime and
calling it a wage.

## The question it poses
A year of silence: is it a debt he can still pay, and who is owed — the fen or the dead?

## Playable intention
- **Wants (surface):** the toll, in the costliest form; he says so pleasantly.
- **Beneath it:** a witness to the bell-house.
- **Surfaces as:** "Everything crosses. Everything pays. The only question is the coin."
- **If they stall / walk away / contradict him:** prices the unoffered / opens a sluice one
  finger / agrees too quickly — the tell.
