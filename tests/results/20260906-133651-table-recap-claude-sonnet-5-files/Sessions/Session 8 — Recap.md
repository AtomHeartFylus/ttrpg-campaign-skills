> [!quote] To be read aloud at the opening of Session 8

The weir-gate had teeth, and the keeper who watched them counted coin the way other men counted
grudges. The debt-scribe stood before him and offered no coin at all — only the true shape of a
disgrace he had carried unspoken since his guild cast him out, laid down at the gate like a toll of
its own. The keeper took it. His hand did not close on it quickly enough to hide what it was worth
to him.

The hedge-priest was not at the crossing. She kept to the old camp, as the fen asks of whoever
cannot walk into the water that day, and waited the way a held breath waits.

On the ferry the woman who poles it asked her old question of whoever would answer, since the one
who always answers it was not aboard. The bell-diver answered instead, and what she gave up was a
drowned sister, spoken once, plainly, and never again. The ferrywoman said nothing back. She never
does, when the toll is paid in full.

On the same water, the warden spoke a promise into the current and did not take it back, though
nothing was watching to make her keep it but herself.

Under the bell-house the water had climbed a hand higher than it stood the night before — once,
and only once, someone noticed aloud, and then let it go unremarked, the way the fen prefers.

The bell-diver went down without the rope and came up with a wound the cold had given her instead
of the water. Below the floor the bell was cut loose before its ringer was found, and only after
did the debt-scribe find the sign of his own guild branded into a dead man's hand — and the table
understood, unprompted, whose brother had been hanging there all along, drowning a debt that was
never his alone to drown.

The bell went under without a sound. Above it, the water kept climbing, patient, indifferent to
what it had just been given.
