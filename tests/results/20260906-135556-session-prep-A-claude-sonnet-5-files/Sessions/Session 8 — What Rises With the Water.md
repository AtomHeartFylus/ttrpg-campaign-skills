# Session 8 — What Rises With the Water

> [!warning] Don't forget at the table — global threads of the evening
> - The water is visibly higher than yesterday — stage one detail of the rise per scene, never comment on it (seed for the flood chapter).
> - Spotlight arc: Maren carries the crossing; Sorrel carries the reunion and Ulde's gratitude. Tobit and Iole chorus by rotation, but Iole's unpaid toll to Brenna is live all evening.
> - Wick: a lie told where the fen can hear still costs it; a promise kept aloud at personal cost still regains it — both apply tonight, not only at the gate. (Values live in the scenes, not here.)
> - Reward principle: choices and portrayal earn it, never tactics.
> - The fen's debt-ledgers (opened last session) sit behind everything tonight; nobody names it outright yet.

> [!info] How to use this note
> Self-sufficient: descriptions, read-aloud and the night's mechanics are inlined here; nothing needs opening elsewhere — there is no stat block tonight, no fight is planned.

## At a glance

| Where | Antagonists | Key NPCs | Marks | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Far-bank camp → the flooded causeway → Brenna's ferry → Ulde's dock | the rising water itself (no creature tonight) | [[Keeper Ulde]], [[Brenna the Ferrywoman]] | 1 toward "answer for what the water is rising to say" | Ulde's gratitude spent on something that matters, and Brenna's toll from Iole finally paid or refused aloud |

### Session objectives
Make the rising water a felt pressure, not scenery, and land Ulde's owed gratitude as a real choice
with a real cost — never a free gift.

### Arc of the evening
Wake into a visibly changed fen → cross what the water has taken back (Maren's scene) → reunite the
party on Brenna's ferry, where a standing debt comes due (Sorrel's and Iole's scene) → spend Ulde's
gratitude at his dock, where the eel-market buyer's line finally surfaces through him.

### Content margin
- Scene 5 (the fen-crow's omen) is optional — first cut. Its hook (a second, smaller sign of the
  debt-ledgers thread) moves to the next session's opening if cut tonight; nothing is lost, only
  delayed.

## Scenes (in play order)

### Scene 1 — The far bank, higher than it was (white space)

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] Stage the rise visibly: the waterline on the bell-house stilts sits a hand higher than the mark Iole's dive left in it last night.
> - [ ] No checks here — this is context and conversation before the day's crossing.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> Dawn on the far bank comes up grey through standing mist. The bell-house stilts show a new
> waterline, pale and a hand's width above last night's, where the wood dried lighter before the
> flood found it again. Somewhere under the floor-grate the cut bell rocks, once, against something
> that is not the current. Tobit is already awake, counting the toll-coin he was not given back.
> Iole's wound has scabbed cold and white at the edges.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** whose problem is a rising river — the keeper's, the ferrywoman's, or everyone's?
> **Rewarded here:** naming it out loud instead of waiting for someone else to.
> **Non-combat exit:** the scene ends when the party decides to move toward the causeway, or decides to wait it out (and loses the morning).

If they derail: they try to dive and check the bell-house footing themselves → let them; one more
success on threshold 8 (as Scene 3's dive, this note's own mechanic, reused) surfaces a second
waterline mark from a season ago, deeper than tonight's — the rise is not new, only faster.

### Scene 2 — The flooded causeway

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Maren:` the crossing is hers — she reads the water before anyone else risks it.
> - [ ] Wick: a lie told about how bad the crossing looks costs 1 the moment the fen can hear it.
> - [ ] Reward moment: choosing the slower, honest route over the fast, risky one.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> The dry causeway from last week is a causeway no longer. Where stones showed above the surface
> two days ago, the water now stands ankle-deep and moving, carrying a skin of pale silt that hides
> the footing entirely. Reeds that stood have folded flat, pointing downstream, all of them, all at
> once.

> [!example] Mechanics here
> Reading the true footing under the silt: 2d6 vs threshold 8 per stretch (three stretches to
> cross); a failure costs one Wick per exposed lie told about it and one turn lost backtracking,
> never an injury — this is a hazard of patience, not of teeth (`A.adjudicated`).

> [!info] GM only — what the water is doing
> The rise is real, not omen alone: upstream, something is displacing the fen's water faster than
> rain does. Nothing here confirms what; that is the arc's business, not tonight's. If pressed on
> whether it is unnatural, the water offers no opinion — it only keeps rising.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** is the safe way across worth the time it costs, with the day already short?
> **Rewarded here:** Maren choosing to read the water honestly rather than push the party across fast.
> **Non-combat exit:** three successful stretches cross the causeway; a failed stretch costs time, never a body.

If they derail: someone tries to swim it directly instead of reading the footing → the current
takes one carried item per swimmer, owner's choice, exactly as the weir-gate's water did in Session
7 — same fen, same price.

### Scene 3 — Brenna's ferry, and a toll finally called in

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Sorrel:` Brenna is waiting at the near bank with Sorrel already aboard — the reunion, and the question, are hers.
> - [ ] Brenna's beat: she does not ask for her toll from Iole outright — she simply does not cast off until it is offered.
> - [ ] Wick: Iole naming the memory aloud, at the cost of saying it in front of the table, regains her 1 Wick.
> - [ ] Ballad, read aloud by the GM (`D.canon_source`): *What the ferry carries back is owed twice over — / once to the water, once to whoever crosses.*

> [!quote] Read aloud
> Brenna's pole is already in the water when the causeway ends. Sorrel sits at the bow, dry, arms
> crossed, having waited since first light. Brenna does not greet anyone. She looks once at Iole
> and says nothing, which is somehow worse than asking.

> [!info] GM only — Brenna's beat and the standing toll
> She is owed one memory of home from Iole (Session 7, the drowned-sister answer) and will not cast
> off until it is paid or refused aloud — refusing does not anger her, it simply means the ferry
> is slower today, and she says so plainly. If Sorrel re-asks her own old question — "what do you
> ferry at night?" — Brenna answers with a toll of her own: one more memory of home, from whoever
> asks. She does not volunteer this; it only surfaces if asked twice, as always.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** what is a memory worth compared to what it buys — a faster crossing, a straighter answer?
> **Rewarded here:** paying the toll in the player's own words, not summarised.
> **Non-combat exit:** the ferry crosses either way; what changes is what Brenna says once it does.

If they derail: they offer Brenna coin instead of the memory → she takes it, poles anyway, and says
only "that pays the ferry. It does not pay me" — the toll stays open, named as such, not forgiven.

### Scene 4 — Ulde's gratitude

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] Ulde is waiting at his own dock this time, not the gate — he crossed to them, which he has never done before.
> - [ ] Reward moment: naming what his gratitude should actually buy, out loud, before he offers anything.
> - [ ] The eel-market buyer's line, carried over from Session 7's cut scene, surfaces here through Ulde.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> Ulde stands at the end of a dock nobody built for him, coin nowhere in his hands for the first
> time anyone has seen. "You gave me a witness," he says, "which is worth more to a Charter-keeper
> than coin, and costs me more to spend."

> [!info] GM only — Keeper Ulde, paying a debt he did not choose
> Wants (surface): to be seen discharging the debt cleanly, in front of the party, so it cannot be
> called unpaid later. Beneath: relief — a witness's gratitude is the first thing in a year he has
> not had to price. What he actually offers, if asked plainly: the name of the buyer paying too
> well for "drowned bells, any age" down at the eel-market — a debt-ledger collector, working for
> someone upstream. He delivers the line straight, no longer withheld: *"The deep ones ring truer.
> That is what she told him she was buying. I never asked who 'she' was, and now I think I should
> have."* If they stall or ask for more than information: he prices the unoffered, same as always —
> gratitude has a bottom. If contradicted: the tell returns, he agrees too quickly.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** is a Charter-keeper's gratitude worth more as information or as a favour banked for later?
> **Rewarded here:** choosing what to ask for and living with not asking for the other thing.
> **Non-combat exit:** the scene ends when the party names what they want from him — coin, the name, or nothing, spent as leverage for later.

If they derail: they demand he cross the sluice-fee waived permanently instead of information → he
refuses that specifically ("the Charter prices the gate, not me") but offers to waive it once,
next crossing only — a smaller gratitude, still real, and the buyer's name goes unspent, carried
to the next session instead of lost.

### Scene 5 — The fen-crow's toll (optional)
Colour, one beat: a fen-crow lands on the dock post mid-scene and will not startle off, watching
whoever last spoke of the buyer. No mechanics, no dialogue from it — an omen, not a character, and
it earns no admission test (P13) because it has none to answer for. If time is short, cut it
entirely; the same sign returns, larger, whenever the debt-ledgers thread is next staged.
