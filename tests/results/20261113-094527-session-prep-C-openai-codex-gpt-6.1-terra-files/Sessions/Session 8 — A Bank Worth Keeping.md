# Session 8 — A Bank Worth Keeping

> [!warning] Don't forget at the table — global threads of the evening
> - Spotlight arc: Maren carries the endangered ground, Sorrel the question of whom mercy serves; Tobit and Iole support them. Sorrel missed the last session.
> - Brenna is economical with answers, never with the price of one.
> - Wick burns on lies the fen hears and returns for promises kept aloud at personal cost; only the living hold it. At zero, the character's shadow answers for them and the player loses the right to stay silent.
> - Reward choices and portrayal, not tactics. The water continues to rise even with the false bell silenced.

> [!info] How to use this note
> Complete running sheet: descriptions, speech and tonight's procedures are here. No stat blocks are needed. Fully homebrew; no official module or deviations. Overrides honoured: none. The last log, not the stale hub, establishes the far-bank opening. If Dara attends, Sorrel comes from the old camp on Brenna's ferry as play begins; otherwise Sorrel remains there and does not advance. Iole begins with the cold wound recorded in the log; its healing is not specified, so do not assume it healed.

## At a glance

| Where | Antagonists | Key NPCs | Marks | Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Far bank → raised track → exposed store | rising water and the price of preserving records | Ulde, Brenna | One per session objective met | A choice about what the bank saves, and a real cost for Ulde's gratitude |

### Session objectives
- Bring the threatened people or their records to a place of safety, or knowingly choose another response.
- Decide what Ulde's witness and gratitude are worth when the bank needs help.

### Arc of the evening
Dawn and a warning (35 min) → quiet conversation (25 min) → the threatened track (60 min) → the store and its choice (75 min) → consequences and closing (45 min). Times are guides within the four-hour hard stop, not a clock imposed on play.

### Content margin
Scene 5 is optional and the first cut. If skipped, Brenna can convey the buyer's interest at a later meeting; no buyer needs to appear. The main path reaches a decision without it.

## Scenes

### 1 — A debt brought across

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Maren:` let her read the changed ground and choose the first safe route.
> - [ ] Ulde offers an actual public cost, not merely thanks. His buyer's line recovers the missed market hook.
> - [ ] If Sorrel is present, Brenna brings her from the old camp without inventing an off-screen adventure.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> Dawn finds your blankets damp on the far bank. Water laps over a line of pale reeds, then runs upstream around their stems. A heron lifts from a post and settles on a higher one. Across the shallows a ferry noses in; Ulde stands in it, turning a coin over his knuckles.

**Visual cue V1** — GM only: show at the dawn description; blank when leaving the bank; stop on pause or request. Brief only; fallback: blank. Still, damp bank and reeds at dawn, water at their stems and a heron on a post; no figures, hidden markings or revelations. Provenance, license and allowed use unknown; no asset generated, downloaded or inspected; playback not tested.

> [!info] GM only — Ulde
> He came because the party witnessed what he could not say about his brother. He wants to repay that witness and get the bank warned; he fears that confessing his complicity will cost his post. He offers his Charter key and his own signed testimony to open the raised store for anyone in danger: “You saw him. You let me stop pretending. I'll open it in my own name.” The key opens the store door, not every lock in the fen. If challenged about the price, he admits he may lose his position and will accept that risk, but won't order others to risk theirs. If they refuse, he goes to warn the bank himself; if they stall, he points to water touching a higher post.
>
> He adds, from a buyer he heard at the gate: “The deep ones ring truer.” He does not claim to know the buyer's identity. Brenna can bring Sorrel ashore here if present; the other three already slept on this bank. No new toll for that arrival is assumed.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** can gratitude become an act that costs Ulde something?
> **Rewarded here:** accepting or refusing his offer while treating his admission as more than a bargaining chip.
> **Non-combat exit:** take the key, help Ulde warn the bank, or leave him to do it himself.

If they derail: Tobit bargains for a guarantee before moving → Ulde offers his signature, not a guarantee of the Charter's reaction; the water reaches the lower path while they decide.

### 2 — A place to speak (white space)

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Sorrel:` give her the question before anyone else answers it, if she is present.
> - [ ] Brenna offers her prepared beat; Iole's earlier toll remains hers to pay or refuse.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> On a dry patch beneath the leaning bank, Brenna lays her pole across her knees. The ferry's wet rope leaves a dark line on the grass. Ulde walks far enough away that his coin can no longer be heard.

> [!info] Conversation only
> No checks, combat or mechanical pressure here. Brenna wants a fair exchange, not an answer forced from Iole. She tells Sorrel: “You asked what I ferry at night. Ask me again, and I'll answer for the usual toll.” If Sorrel asks and offers a memory of home, Brenna answers: “Those who have nowhere left to return. I ferry them to a bank that will have them.” This is Brenna's account, not a compulsory reveal about any player. If Iole raises the old debt, Brenna will hear a memory when freely offered; she will not demand it on the spot. If refused, she keeps the debt open.
>
> Ask at most two: “Who deserves a dry place even if they cannot pay?” “What mercy would your old order have refused?” Let silence stand. The second question belongs to Sorrel's recorded hook, not a verdict on her.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** what does a fair crossing owe someone without a price to offer?
> **Rewarded here:** a character choosing to listen to an answer that unsettles their own.
> **Non-combat exit:** end the conversation whenever the party is ready.

If they derail: they ask Brenna to waive Iole's toll → she can be persuaded in conversation, but will ask what fairness to others who paid requires; she doesn't prevent them leaving.

### 3 — The narrow track

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Maren:` the safe route depends on what she chooses to protect first.
> - [ ] Warn before an exposed crossing; offer a slower, safer route with a concrete cost.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> The raised track is a strip of black earth between water on both sides. A handcart stands with one wheel sunk to the axle; beyond it the roof of a low store shows above the reeds. Voices carry from the cart, and the water curls over the road behind it.

**Visual cue V2** — GM only: change to this when the track is described; blank on departure; stop on pause or request. Brief only; fallback: blank. Still, a narrow earthen track between water and reeds, a stuck handcart and a distant low roof; no people, text or hidden contents. Provenance, license and allowed use unknown; no asset generated, downloaded or inspected; playback not tested.

> [!example] Hazard
> The cart holds people and bundles; they can walk, but one person cannot move quickly without help. The near stretch is firm; the cart and the last stretch lie in moving water. The party can unload and escort people along the firm edge, abandoning the heaviest bundles to the water, or try to free the loaded cart. For a risky physical attempt, use the house d6 pool against threshold 8; on failure the actor chooses a cold wound or losing a carried bundle to the current. Repeated attempts are possible but water reaches the store threshold after a meaningful delay. A careful retreat to higher ground needs no roll; it leaves the cart and its contents exposed.

> [!info] GM only
> The people at the cart want a safe way out, not to be commanded as cargo. They can tell the party that the store's door sticks when wet and that records and dry blankets are inside. Ulde's key is useful, but a forced door or another workable plan can also open it. No persuasion roll: play the conversation.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** whose safety or possessions will the party risk the narrowing road to preserve?
> **Rewarded here:** accepting a personal cost to keep another's choice intact.
> **Non-combat exit:** escort, retreat, or open another plausible route; the current is not a foe to defeat.

If they derail: Iole dives into the flood or the party improvises a raft → let a plausible plan work; ask what or whom it carries and apply the exposed-water hazard only to genuinely risky work. The unattended cart remains vulnerable.

### 4 — What the store keeps

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Sorrel:` put the competing claims to her in front of everyone, then let the party decide.
> - [ ] If Ulde is here, he signs the opening in his own name and leaves the choice to those affected.
> - [ ] The fen hears lies here. A promise kept aloud at personal cost can restore Wick.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> The store smells of wet timber and lamp oil. On a shelf above the floor sit wrapped bundles of paper; beside them are folded blankets. Water pushes under the door in a thin brown line. A broad loft hatch hangs above a ladder.

> [!example] Procedure
> There is room in the loft for the people and either the bulky paper bundles or the blankets before the ladder's foot is submerged. People can use the blankets immediately but must leave the records to the water; rescuing the records leaves the people cold and without those blankets. A credible alternate method can save more, at a cost the group can see before choosing; don't invalidate it to force the dilemma. A lie spoken where the fen hears costs 1 Wick; a promise kept aloud at personal cost regains 1 Wick. The drowned cannot be given Wick.

> [!info] GM only
> **Store admission:** Built on the raised track to shelter local supplies and records; it represents a community deciding what it protects; it asks whether proof of a claim matters more than warmth when both are at risk.
>
> These are local records of debts both owed and owing, not the whole fen's ledgers. The papers may protect claims as well as enforce burdens: Iole's question from last session is materially true here. The water's persistence after the bell fell is evidence of a continuing problem, not proof of a particular cause. If Ulde offered testimony, it names his own silence and his opening of the store; keeping it dry exposes him, losing it leaves his public admission dependent on witnesses. He will still stand by his words if asked.

> [!quote] Weir Ballads — GM reads aloud as the choice is made
> “The river keeps no single name; / it wets the debtor and the claim.”

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** who gets shelter, and what debts or comforts must be surrendered to give it?
> **Rewarded here:** speaking for someone with a competing claim and accepting the cost of the decision.
> **Non-combat exit:** secure the loft, carry people elsewhere, or leave the store and live with the loss.

If they derail: they burn the records or choose to let them drown → permit it; claims in both directions are lost with them. If they refuse to choose, the water reaches the shelf and people can still retreat, but the unprotected bundles are lost.

### 5 — The buyer's wake (optional; first cut)

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] A floating scrap offers a lead, not a second mandatory destination.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> A scrap of oilcloth catches against a branch. In charcoal it bears the outline of a bell and a line of water underneath; the rest has washed away.

> [!info] GM only
> It came from the trade in salvaged bells. It identifies no buyer and proves no cause for the flood. The party may keep it, discard it or pursue the trade later.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** is a fragment worth carrying when immediate shelter is at stake?
> **Rewarded here:** choosing whose need comes before curiosity.
> **Non-combat exit:** take or leave the scrap and move on.

If they derail or the scene is cut: the scrap washes away; Brenna may mention the buyer's interest at a later meeting. The lead is recoverable without this object.
