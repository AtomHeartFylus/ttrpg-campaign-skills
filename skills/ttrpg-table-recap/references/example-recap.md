> [!quote] To be read aloud at the opening of Session 8

They came to the weir-gate where the water stood a hand too high and said nothing about it, and
a keeper turned a coin he never looked at. Everything crosses, he told them; everything pays.
The debt-scribe paid. Not in coin — in the one thing no purse holds twice: he laid down his
guild-shame on the walkway, plainly, where the fen could hear it, and the gate's seven teeth
came up out of the water for him.

<!-- Traceable to the log: the toll, the regret, the tell. The MECHANIC (1 Wick, threshold 9)
does not exist here (P12). The regret is rendered as a gesture — "laid down his guild-shame,
plainly" — not as psychological gloss ("he bravely opened up"). Show, do not explain. -->

The hedge-priest kept the fire at the old camp, as the custom of the road demands, and the fire
was the better for it.

<!-- The absent player: accounted for by the B.absence convention, in one line, in fiction. The

On the black water the Ferrywoman poled them past the leaning house, and asked her fare in
memories. The bell-diver paid the deepest: a sister the water had already taken once. The ferry
went quiet the way a held breath is quiet. *Below the weir the bells are hung,* say the old
verses, *that upward drown, that downward rung* — and there beneath the floor-grate they found it
true: a bell hung mouth-down into the dark, calling the flood up the way a lie calls another lie.

<!-- The canon quote is woven in — the text arrives at it, the source speaks two lines, the
telling resumes. The individual moment is commemorated at the scale it had: one sentence, the
table's silence rendered as an image, no inflation into heroics. -->

The bell-diver went down to it with a rope and came up with winter in her hands. The Wight that
kept the bell fought for it the way a drowned man holds a door — and when the rope was cut and
the bronze went down mouth-first, it stopped, all at once, like a debt struck through. On the
dead thing's wrist the debt-scribe found a guild-mark he knew, and said the name himself; no one
had to tell him. The keeper of the gate is owed nothing now, and owes more than he knows.

<!-- The central turn takes the longest passage. The fight exists only through its objective (the
bell), never through depletion. The reveal appears exactly as the table earned it — the players
named the brother, so the fiction lets the debt-scribe say the name. -->

They slept on the far bank. Upstream — only a hand's breadth, only that — the water is still
climbing.
