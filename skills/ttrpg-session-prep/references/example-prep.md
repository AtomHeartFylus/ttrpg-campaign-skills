# Session 7 — The Drowned Toll

> [!warning] Don't forget at the table — global threads of the evening
> - Brenna's register tonight: clipped, unpaid-debt politeness — she knows what the bell-house hides.
> - Seed for the next chapter: once per scene, one detail of the water rising *upstream*. Never comment on it.
> - Spotlight arc: Tobit carries the toll and the ledger reveal; Iole carries the bell-house. Maren and Sorrel are chorus tonight (they carried the last session — rotation period 2).
> - Wick tonight: it burns on lies within the fen's hearing; it can come back only on the ferry. No numbers here — costs live in the scenes.
> - Rewards: choices and portrayal, never tactics — tonight above all, refusing Ulde's easy bargain.

### Session objectives
- Get the party across the weir with the ledger page intact.
- Put the false bell in front of them and make silencing it cost something.

### Arc of the evening
Arrival and toll (tight) → the ferry (loose, white space) → the bell-house (the turn) → out on the far bank.

## Scenes (in play order)

### Scene 1 — The toll at the weir-gate

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] Ulde addresses Tobit by his guild-name — he has already read the ledger page. `Spotlight → Tobit:` the negotiation is his.
<!-- VARIANT — B.distance close/self-insert: this focus line would point at a person, not a

> - [ ] Wick: lying to Ulde inside the gate costs 1 Wick — the fen hears everything here. Say the cost *before* the first lie, not after.
> - [ ] Reward moment: paying the toll with a true thing — a real regret, spoken — instead of coin.
> - [ ] If they pay in coin: mark which coin — it surfaces again next session.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> The weir-gate stands with its teeth down: seven sluice-blades, green to the waterline. On the
> walkway a squat figure turns a coin over his knuckles without looking at it. The water on the
> upstream side sits a hand higher than it should, and it is quiet — quieter than moving water
> has any right to be.

<!-- P3: senses only. WHY the water is rising (the false bell calls it) is in the GM note of
Scene 3, seeded through clues — never announced in read-aloud. -->

> [!example] Mechanics here
> - Reading Ulde's ledger unnoticed: 3d6 vs threshold 9 — threshold 7 if Tobit invokes his
>   guild-name first. One attempt; on a failure Ulde names the price aloud.
> - Toll options: two marks' worth of coin · one true regret, spoken · one favour owed, recorded.

> [!info] GM only — Keeper Ulde
> **Wants (surface):** the toll, in the form that costs the payer most — and he says so, pleasantly.
> **Beneath it:** a witness. He has kept the bell-house's secret for a year and it is drowning
> him; he will not say it, but he steers anyone stubborn toward the bell-house.
> **Surfaces as:** "Everything crosses. Everything pays. The only question is the coin." — and the
> gesture: he never once looks at the coin he keeps turning.
> **If they stall:** he starts pricing things they did not offer ("Your warden's silence — what is
> that worth?"). **If they walk away:** he lets them, and opens a sluice one finger — the water
> says what he will not. **If they contradict him:** he agrees too quickly. That is the tell.

<!-- P4, full box, earned: misreading Ulde (a shakedown instead of a plea) kills the only route
to the secret. Note both layers are playable and the surface want has a speakable line. -->

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** what is the toll actually *for* — and is Ulde collecting it, or paying it?
> **Rewarded here:** paying in truth; treating Ulde as a person and not a tollbooth.
> **Non-combat exit:** convince or pay — the gate opens on either. Fighting Ulde closes the gate
> for the season; say it through Brenna if they reach for weapons.

<!-- VARIANT — `P5 — off` in E.overrides: this compass block and the exit condition drop from

If they derail: they try to swim or climb the weir → the fen itself is the pressure: the quiet
water takes one carried item per swimmer, chosen by its owner. The gate stays the only dry crossing.

> [!quote] The Weir Ballads — read when the bell-house appears
> *Below the weir the bells are hung, / that upward drown, that downward rung.*

Context: the crossing takes as long as the table wants it to. Trigger questions — use two at most:
- "Who taught you what a fair price is?" (Brenna, to whoever paid the toll)
- "What do you each *not* look at as the bell-house passes?"

If they derail: they press Brenna about Ulde → she answers exactly one question about him, then
poles in silence. The silence is the answer.

### Scene 3 — The bell-house

> [!todo] Don't miss in this scene
> - [ ] `Spotlight → Iole:` the dive is hers; stage the underwater bell through her senses only.
> - [ ] The Bell-Wight will **not** strike whoever carries the ledger page — visible hesitation, first pass.
> - [ ] Wick: cutting the false bell loose while it rings costs 1 Wick (it rings the lie in your own voice). State the cost when the rope is in hand.
> - [ ] Reward moment: choosing to drown the bell rather than keep it — it is plainly worth three marks of coin.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> The bell-house leans on four black stilts. Under the floor-grate a bell hangs *down* into the
> water, mouth beneath the surface — and the water around its lip is climbing in a slow coil,
> upstream. Something below keeps time against the bronze with what sounds like a knuckle.

> [!example] Mechanics here
> - The false bell, while ringing: every lie told within earshot costs 1 additional Wick.
> - Cutting it loose: two successes at threshold 8 on the rope, working underwater; each failure
>   is one wound — from the cold, not from the Wight.
> - Bell-Wight local trait: it strikes only at whoever last spoke an untruth. Full block:
>   [[Bell-Wight — stats]].

<!-- The typed enemy's local trait is inlined where it is needed; the stat block stays a link —
the single exception P1 admits. -->

> [!info] GM only
> The bell was hung by Ulde's brother to call the water up and drown the fen's debt-ledgers.
> Seed it, never say it: the knuckle-rhythm under the water matches Ulde's coin-turning gesture
> from Scene 1. The brother is dead under the bell; the Wight wears his hands. Do not name him —
> let Tobit find the guild-mark on the wrist.

> [!abstract] Dramatic compass
> **Question:** the bell drowns the fen's debts along with the fen — who at this table thinks
> that is a bad trade?
> **Rewarded here:** having that argument in character, with the water rising.
> **Non-combat exit:** the fight ends when the bell is *drowned or cut loose*, not when the Wight
> is depleted — it stops the instant the bell stops. Show its exit condition through behaviour:
> it defends the rope, it never pursues past the grate.

If they derail: they decide to *let* the bell ring and drown the ledgers → do not block it. The
water rises for real: the next chapter becomes the flood chapter, and Ulde's crossing becomes the
last dry road in the fen. A cost, not a wall.
